Status: in progress, updated daily.

The brief
Hackathon topic: “AI-based demand forecasting for car sales”. The product is an agentic pipeline: a research agent finds public sources for data gaps; the system fetches and archives them with the URL, fetch date and content hash; extracts values with their source location; validates and publishes versioned data; forecasts against honest baselines and flags large errors; and sends changes to a human review screen. A conversational agent answers questions over forecasts and evidence, and a core feature forecasts a newly launched model from comparable analog models.

Project framing and ground rules Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
A public hackathon, topic “AI-based demand forecasting for car sales”, with a proof of concept due 3 Dec 2026. The project builds on lessons from earlier internal forecasting work, and planning is run with an AI coding agent: a skill-driven flow of setup, grill-with-docs, research, prototype, spec, then tickets. Before any code, the agent produces a questionnaire of 20 open decisions in 9 groups (scope, data, ingestion, forecasting, agents, UI, evaluation, quality, packaging).
Options
(A) Start building and decide as problems appear. (B) Write down every open decision first, each with options, trade-offs, a recommended default and the document it feeds, then answer them one at a time. (C) Reuse the earlier internal code and figures as a head start.
Decision
Option B for process and a hard no on C. Stack leaning: Python, FastAPI and Pydantic; React and Vite; LangGraph for orchestration and human-review interrupts; DuckDB, Parquet and SQLite for data and run state; an OpenAI-compatible LLM client that can switch between hosted providers such as OpenRouter and NVIDIA NIM through configuration. New-model launch forecasting is a core feature, not a stretch goal.
Basis
Grounding rule: every forecasting figure must come from public data or a run inside this project. Earlier work may guide direction as general lessons only: no numbers, names, configurations or code carry over. The first data are public Mazda U.S. sales and model-year releases plus the FuelEconomy.gov dataset.
Implication
Decisions made in the open are cheaper to change than decisions buried in code. The clean-room rule keeps the project publishable and removes any doubt about where a number came from. The cost is that the project starts with less data than a reuse would have given.
FDE skill learned
  • Clean-room boundary. When you reuse a past client or employer engagement, carry over lessons, not artefacts. Write the rule down at the start so every downstream document respects it.
  • Decision-first planning. Before writing code, list every open decision with its options, trade-offs, a recommended default and the document it feeds. Vague scope becomes reviewable choices, and the questionnaire doubles as the first design doc. Roadmap topic: Technical demos and one-page design docs →

Q1: What do we mean by “demand”? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
The topic says demand forecasting, but the only public ground truth is reported monthly unit sales. Nobody publishes demand directly.
Options
(A) Forecast reported sales and call it a demand proxy. (B) Model unconstrained demand separately. (A + B-lite) Score sales only, and add a clearly labelled, unscored demand view built from public signals.
Decision
A + B-lite. Working definition: demand = observed sales + sales lost to constraints (stock-outs, launch delays) + intent that has not converted yet. Only observed sales are scored. The demand view is shown with stated assumptions. Each signal enters as a dated feature and is tested by ablation on the same blind backtest origins.
Basis
Public signals checked on 19 Nov 2026:
  • Cox Automotive vAuto Live Market View: monthly national new-vehicle inventory, days’ supply, sales and listing price (CSV), with brand and model days’ supply in the monthly articles. Low days’ supply suggests sales were capped by stock.
  • U. Michigan Surveys of Consumers, Table 37: monthly “good or bad time to buy a vehicle”, broken down by age, income and education. Table 38 gives the reasons.
  • NY Fed SCE Household Spending Survey: percent chance of buying a car in the next four months, published every four months.
  • Google Trends per nameplate, filterable by U.S. state, as a localised interest signal.
Mazda U.S. press releases supply the sales ground truth and launch dates.
Implication
Judges get an honest, on-topic demand story while the scored claim stays verifiable. The price is more ingestion adapters and careful point-in-time handling: each input must have been public by the forecast date, so survey and inventory releases need vintages, not just latest values.
FDE skill learned
  • Translate the client’s word into a measurable target. “Demand” in a brief usually cannot be observed directly. Define it, separate what you can score from what you can only estimate, and label the estimate. Roadmap topic: Customer discovery and scoping a pilot →
  • Proxy plus signals. Use the observable outcome as ground truth, and add leading and constraint indicators as features you can falsify through ablation on the same blind test. Roadmap topic: Agent evaluation and observability →

Q2: How far ahead do we score the forecast? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
A horizon is how far ahead the forecast looks. Scoring one month ahead means that at the end of October you predict November and compare it with the reported figure. Every extra scored month costs test points: each blind origin needs that many future months of actuals, so the latest origins drop out. Errors also grow with horizon. Planners allocate and order stock months ahead, so a one-month-only forecast is less useful for planning.
Options
(A) Score one month ahead as the headline, and report months 2 and 3 as secondary, unscored metrics. (B) Require months 1 to 3 to be scored.
Decision
Optimise for at least a three-month horizon: the headline score covers horizons 1 to 3, judged on the three-month total and the per-horizon errors. Six months is checked as a secondary view where the data allows it.
Basis
Business usefulness. Allocation and production planning work on a three-to-six-month lead time, so a one-month score alone does not help a planner, however clean it looks statistically.
Implication
To keep enough scored origins, the blind window moves earlier. Models must forecast multi-step (direct per-horizon or recursive), which favours global models and models that output a full horizon. Error is reported by horizon, not only pooled, because it is expected to grow with lead time. The scored horizon is fixed before any tuning so the headline result cannot be cherry-picked.
FDE skill learned
  • Choose the horizon by the decision it feeds, not the metric that looks best. A forecast is only as useful as its lead time relative to the client’s planning cycle. Roadmap topic: Customer discovery and scoping a pilot →
  • Every requirement has an evaluation cost. A longer horizon shrinks the usable test window; state that trade-off explicitly and lock the protocol before tuning. Roadmap topic: Agent evaluation and observability →

Q3–Q5: Reproducible ingestion, partial-data fixtures and discovery tests (Mazda phase) Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
The first data source was the Mazda USA newsroom, whose monthly sales releases had already been scraped, cleaned and conditioned with hand-written scripts. Two kinds of failure had come up: some months publish the sales table only as an image, and one release names just a few nameplates plus the national total. The questions were how to make ingestion reproducible, which months become test fixtures, and how to test whether an agent can find sources on its own.
Options
Ingestion: (A) a frozen local archive with hashes and a manifest; (B) keep URLs and re-fetch. Fixtures: synthetic edge cases, or the real months that already broke the scraper. Discovery tests: hide a product or launch release, hide sales releases, or both.
Decision
A hybrid for ingestion: the existing scraper and archive are the seed, and the research agent gets the same capabilities as typed tools (discover release pages, scrape, parse, clean) so it can replicate and extend the results, with a human filling gaps such as image-only tables. The two real failure months became fixtures: an image-only table that must be transcribed and reconciled to the totals stated in the release text (recovery), and a release that names only some nameplates, where unreported rows must stay blank (abstention). Discovery is tested both ways, with sales-release rediscovery as the primary suite and a hidden launch release as the secondary one.
Basis
The workflow had already been proven by hand, so the agent productises it, and archived bytes keep it auditable. The failure months come with verified answers. Sales releases are the ground truth everything depends on, so a missed or misread release is the most damaging failure. One detail worth recording: the first recollection of which months had failed was off by four years; the execution log was right.
Implication
Image extraction must reconcile against totals in the text, and the sales table holds parent and child rows, so naive summing double counts. Answer keys are locked before any agent run. In a real rollout dealer data would be daily or weekly, so the schema and pipeline are kept frequency-agnostic.
FDE skill learned
  • Automate what you have already done by hand. Do the task manually once, then turn each manual step into an agent tool with a typed output and a human check. The manual run becomes the agent’s answer key. Roadmap topic: How an AI agent calls a tool →
  • Your own failures make the best fixtures, and logs beat memory. Edge cases you already hit and solved come with verified answers. Check facts against artefacts before they go into requirements.
  • Hide-and-rediscover evals. Remove known evidence, have the agent find it again, and score coverage, accuracy, abstention and cost. Point the hardest test at the riskiest dependency. Roadmap topic: Agent evaluation and observability →

Q6: How often do we poll sources? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
The manufacturer posts monthly sales about once a month, usually on the first business day, but timing and URLs vary.
Options
(A) Daily checks. (B) Hourly checks. (C) Sparse background checks plus a burst around the expected release date.
Decision
C. Check every two weeks, plus daily on the first five days of each month until that month’s release is found, then stop the burst. A release still missing after day five raises a review alert instead of failing silently.
Basis
The schedule mirrors how the source actually publishes. The burst covers releases delayed by weekends or holidays without wasting requests; the fortnightly checks catch corrections and off-cycle posts.
Implication
The scheduler needs state (“have we found month M yet?”). Cadence is configuration, so a denser feed such as daily dealer data can change it later.
FDE skill learned
  • Match ingestion cadence to the source. Poll on the publisher’s rhythm, use event-driven stop conditions, and alert on missed SLAs rather than failing quietly. Roadmap topic: Rate limiting and background jobs → Roadmap topic: SLOs, error budgets and incident handling →

Q7: Which series and metrics carry the headline claim? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
There are many series (brand, nameplate and below). One headline claim is needed against the seasonal naive and four-month moving average (MA4) baselines, without letting big sellers hide misses on small ones.
Options
Series: (A) brand total only; (B) pooled, volume-weighted WAPE across nameplates; (C) an unweighted per-nameplate average. Metrics: WAPE/WMAPE, MAE, WRMSSE, MASE/RMSSE.
Decision
Two lenses plus a guardrail. Headline: pooled WAPE across nameplates for horizons 1 to 3. Fairness lens: per-series scaled error (MASE/RMSSE) reported by volume tier. Guardrail: beat both baselines in every volume tier. WRMSSE across hierarchy levels is a secondary check. Discontinued nameplates leave scoring after their last sales month (forecasting sales after that is a pass/fail flag), and new launches are scored on a separate launch track against an analog baseline for their first three months.
Basis
Volume-weighted metrics are dominated by the best sellers. Scaling each series by its own naive error judges low sellers against their own difficulty. Zero actuals make scaled metrics divide by zero and hand out free wins, and launches lack the history the baselines need.
Implication
Volume tiers need definitions, and recently launched models need a fallback baseline (a moving average over the months available, or analog seasonality) until they have twelve months of history.
FDE skill learned
  • Pick metrics that match the claim. Know the difference between volume-weighted and scaled errors, evaluate across hierarchy levels, and decide up front how series that start or stop inside the window are scored. Roadmap topic: Agent evaluation and observability →

Data pivot and Q8: Primary dataset and granularity below model level Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
Mazda was a placeholder. Public research compared three candidates: Kia America’s monthly per-model U.S. sales (a long archive, roughly 50–80 thousand units a month and many launches); RDW Netherlands open registration data (version codes and list prices, but registrations of surviving vehicles and no regions); and New York DMV registrations decoded with NHTSA’s vPIC VIN decoder (daily and trim-decodable, but only recent years).
Options
(A) Kia U.S. as primary, scored at model level, with trim from estimated shares. (B) RDW Kia Netherlands as primary for a true trim-level hierarchy, with Kia U.S. as a second proof. (C) Stay on Mazda.
Decision
Switch to Kia. Kia U.S. model-level monthly sales are the scored core, and the agentic platform (research agent, ingestion, the learning loop, launch forecasting) is the main product. Trim, feature and price inputs come from public sources as features, not as a scored hierarchy. A trim-level forecast on RDW is an optional side proof if time allows.
Basis
The deadline was two weeks out. Manufacturer-published U.S. data is complete, reliable and real, while version-to-trim mapping and survivorship correction in RDW are slow to get right. The judging panel is not focused on hierarchical forecasting, although an OEM would care about it in production.
Implication
The Q3–Q5 fixtures and answer keys had to be redone for Kia, the site’s terms had to be checked before any bulk download, the Q7 tiers now apply to Kia models, and launch analogs come from the Kia line-up.
FDE skill learned
  • Scope the deliverable to the deadline and the judging criteria. Separate the scored core from showcase extras, and choose data by reliability and completeness rather than granularity alone. Roadmap topic: Customer discovery and scoping a pilot →

Q9: Which launches prove new-model forecasting? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
New-model launch forecasting is a core requirement. It is proven by replaying real Kia launches with everything after launch hidden.
Options
Telluride 2019 (a breakout hit), Seltos 2020 (launched into the COVID shock), EV9 2023 (a new segment with weak analogs), K4 2024 (a direct Forte replacement).
Decision
Three replays, each testing a different analog type: K4 against its predecessor, EV9 against same-segment and feature and price-band analogs, and Telluride as the stress test for a breakout hit.
Basis
Together they cover predecessor, segment and attribute analogs and the easy, hard and outlier difficulty levels. Showing where the method succeeds and where it struggles is more credible than one cherry-picked win.
Implication
Each replay hides post-launch data, locks its answer key first, scores the first three months and records the analog choice and the planner’s approval in the evidence trail. Telluride is expected to miss, and the demo explains why.
FDE skill learned
  • Honest evaluation for cold-start forecasting. Choose analog strategies deliberately and show failure modes on purpose. Roadmap topic: Agent evaluation and observability → Roadmap topic: Technical demos and one-page design docs →

Q10: Where does agent run state live? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
Agent runs pause for human review (for example a planner approving an analog days later) and must resume reliably.
Options
(A) LangGraph checkpoints only. (B) Custom SQLite run tables only. (C) Both, each with its own job.
Decision
A. LangGraph checkpoints own all execution state, and inspection uses the framework’s own APIs: state and state history per thread, listing checkpoints to find runs paused at an interrupt, and typed interrupt payloads so the review screen can read what is waiting. Evidence and published forecasts stay in the data layer, apart from the runtime.
Basis
Keep the execution runtime isolated from the data layer. A custom abstraction would be a mess to build in two weeks, while the framework’s persistence is tried and tested.
Implication
Define a typed state schema and typed interrupt payloads up front so stored state is readable, and pin the framework version because checkpoint formats can change.
FDE skill learned
  • Separate the execution layer from the data layer. Use a framework’s persistence and introspection APIs instead of reinventing them. Roadmap topic: Clean / hexagonal architecture → Roadmap topic: Agent memory and multi-agent orchestration →

Q11: How does the system learn from reviews? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
Planner corrections (rejected extractions, swapped analogs, overridden forecasts) are the signal for the learning loop.
Options
(A) Log them only. (B) Turn corrections into stored rules or few-shot examples the agent reads next time. (C) Retrain or reweight models from corrections.
Decision
B. Each correction is stored as a typed, versioned rule or example with its scope (model, segment, task), reason, reviewer and date, and retrieved on future runs. A rule goes live only after a blind backtest shows it does not make things worse; a rule that replaces or overrides an existing one also needs a human review and a proven improvement.
Basis
Logging alone never improves during the demo; retraining costs too much and risks overfitting to one reviewer. Stored rules improve right away and every change is explainable.
Implication
Conflicting rules are flagged for review, every forecast cites the rules it used, and rules can be disabled and rolled back.
FDE skill learned
  • Human-in-the-loop learning without retraining. Treat agent memory as governed, testable configuration with review gates and rollback. Roadmap topic: Agent memory and multi-agent orchestration → Roadmap topic: Fine-tuning vs prompting vs RAG →

Q12: What may the conversational agent do? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
A conversational agent sits on top of forecasts, evidence and the pipeline.
Options
(A) Read-only Q&A. (B) Q&A plus starting jobs: research runs, re-forecasts and what-if scenarios. (C) Full control, including approvals, publishing and rule edits.
Decision
B. A tool-using agent for planners and dealers that answers with citations and can start research, re-forecast and what-if jobs.
Basis
It delivers the most value and the strongest demo. Full control would let the agent approve its own work, breaking the human-review principle from Q11.
Implication
Approvals, publishing and rule activation stay human-only. Jobs the agent starts get cost and run limits and are logged, and tools are role-scoped so each user sees only their own scope.

Q13: What triggers a proactive alert? Decided

19 Nov 2026

Context
“Pre-emptive sales analysis” means the system flags issues before planners ask, without flooding them with noise.
Options
Forecast-versus-actual drift; shifts in signals such as inventory, sentiment or search; source events; model health.
Decision
Severe drift, relative to each model’s normal volatility, opens a review that classifies the cause as model error or an external event; event-caused misses are tagged and do not create learned rules. Sentiment and demand signals are features shown as context inside reviews, not triggers. Incentives, seasonal discounts and other events are entered by planners as dated flags that feed the what-if engine. Backtest metrics are monitored continuously and any drop against the baselines raises an alert.
Basis
This separates model accountability from external shocks, keeps alerts few and actionable, and leaves humans in charge of judging the cause.
Implication
An event-flag schema (type, date range, scope, source, entered by), per-model volatility bands and a review state for event classification.
FDE skill learned
  • Alert design. Distinguish actionable triggers from explanatory signals, attribute misses to model or event, and guard against alert fatigue. Roadmap topic: Logging and monitoring → Roadmap topic: SLOs, error budgets and incident handling →

Q14: How is the blind backtest run? Decided

20 Nov 2026

Context
Rolling-origin backtests on Kia U.S. monthly per-model sales, horizons 1 to 3.
Options
How many origins; how to treat distorted periods such as COVID and the chip shortage; all history versus a recent fixed training window; who controls blindness.
Decision
Twelve origins by default. Distorted periods are kept and flagged as anomaly events with a signed impact weight used as a feature, sharing one schema with what-if levers such as a discount. Training uses up to the last five years, with longer windows tested only as a variant. Model and configuration versions are kept under version control so a known-good version can be restored while the next is tuned.
Basis
