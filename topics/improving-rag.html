Improving RAG: chunking, hybrid search, reranking, query rewriting, GraphRAG

Track: AI/LLM systems. Drop 3 showed how to measure RAG; this is what to change when the scores are bad.
Most bad RAG answers are retrieval failures: the right paragraph never reached the model. Improving RAG means fixing the pipeline in layers, and measuring each change against your golden set (drop 3, topic 2).

What it is and the problem it solves

A basic RAG pipeline splits documents into fixed-size chunks, embeds them, and returns the top 5 by vector similarity. It breaks in predictable ways: a chunk loses its context (\"revenue rose 14%\" but which company, which quarter?), exact terms like error codes, part numbers or names are missed because embeddings capture meaning, not spelling; the right chunk is retrieved at position 30 and never shown to the model; the user's question is vague or has three questions in one; and questions that need connecting many documents (\"what are the main themes across all incident reports?\") cannot be answered by any single chunk.

How it works

  1. 1. Chunking (at indexing time)Split along the document's own structure (headings, sections, table rows, slides) rather than every 500 characters; keep a little overlap; attach the title and heading path to each chunk. Anthropic's 'Contextual Retrieval' goes further: an LLM writes a 50-100 token note saying where each chunk sits in the document, and that note is prepended before embedding and keyword indexing.
  2. 2. Hybrid searchRun two searches at once: BM25 keyword search (great for exact words) and vector search (great for meaning). Merge the two ranked lists, usually with Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF), which rewards chunks that rank well in either list.
  3. 3. RerankingFirst-stage search compares pre-computed vectors, so it is fast but rough. A reranker (cross-encoder) reads the question and each candidate chunk together and scores true relevance. Retrieve wide (e.g. 150), rerank, pass the best 5-20 to the LLM. Better answers, and fewer tokens = lower cost.
  4. 4. Query rewritingBefore searching, an LLM turns the user's message into better searches: resolve 'it' from chat history, expand acronyms, generate several phrasings (multi-query), or split a compound question into sub-questions and retrieve for each. HyDE writes a hypothetical answer and searches with that.
  5. 5. GraphRAG (when needed)An LLM extracts entities and relationships from the corpus into a knowledge graph and summarises clusters (communities) of related entities. Questions then retrieve connected sub-graphs or community summaries, which answers 'across everything' and multi-hop questions that chunk search cannot.
Everyday comparison: a good librarian.
Chunking is how the books are shelved and labelled (a page torn out with no title is useless). Hybrid search is the librarian checking both the exact-title catalogue and 'books about this topic'. Reranking is the librarian skimming the 50 books on the cart and handing you the best 5. Query rewriting is the librarian asking 'do you mean the tax form or the tax law?' before searching. GraphRAG is the librarian who has read everything and drawn a map of who is connected to whom.

When to use / when not to use

Solid arrows = yes, dashed = no. Order of effort: chunking and hybrid search are cheap and almost always worth it; reranking adds ~100-300 ms and a per-query cost; GraphRAG is expensive to build and refresh, so use it only for question types that need it.

Pattern card: Layered retrieval (hybrid + rerank)

What it is
Retrieve wide with keyword + vector search, fuse, then rerank to a small precise set before the LLM sees it.
Metaphor
Two catalogues and a librarian who skims the cart before handing you the best five books.
Real example
Anthropic Contextual Retrieval; LinkedIn's knowledge-graph RAG for customer service (below). Azure AI Search 'hybrid + semantic ranker' is the same idea as a managed feature.
Use when
Evals show low recall@20 or noisy context; corpora with exact identifiers; anything customer-facing.
Do not use when
A few dozen short documents fit in the context window (just send them, possibly with prompt caching).
Trade-offs
Better answers and fewer tokens, in exchange for re-indexing, reranker latency and per-query cost.
Instruction to give AI or a teammate
Re-chunk by document headings with 10% overlap and prepend the title and heading path to every chunk, switch retrieval to hybrid BM25 + vector with RRF returning the top 100, add a reranker that keeps the top 8, and report recall@20 and faithfulness before and after on our golden set.

Real companies: Anthropic and LinkedIn

Anthropic's 'Contextual Retrieval' post (Sept 2024) measured these layers across codebases, fiction, arXiv papers and science papers. Using the share of relevant chunks missing from the top 20 as the failure rate: contextual embeddings cut failures by 35% (5.7% to 3.7%), adding contextual BM25 (hybrid) cut them by 49% (to 2.9%), and adding reranking cut them by 67% (to 1.9%). They also note that for small knowledge bases (under about 200k tokens) you can skip RAG and put everything in the prompt.

LinkedIn's SIGIR 2024 paper 'Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Knowledge Graphs for Customer Service Question Answering' built a knowledge graph from historical support tickets (each ticket as a tree of sections, tickets linked to related tickets) instead of treating tickets as flat text chunks. It beat their baseline by 77.6% in MRR, and after about six months in LinkedIn's customer service team it cut median per-issue resolution time by 28.6%.

Why it matters on the job: the most common FDE request after a RAG demo is 'it gives wrong answers'. Knowing which layer to fix, and proving it with evals, is the difference between guessing and engineering.

Interview angle

  • 'Our RAG bot gives wrong answers. How do you fix it?' Strong answer: build or reuse a golden set, split retrieval vs generation failures, then fix retrieval in layers (chunking, hybrid, rerank, query rewriting), change one thing at a time, and gate releases on recall and faithfulness.
  • 'When would you use GraphRAG?' Strong answer: global or multi-hop questions across many documents, or data that is naturally linked (tickets, entities, org charts); not for simple lookups, because graph extraction and refresh are costly.

Glossary

Chunk / chunking
A piece of a document stored for search; chunking is how you split documents into pieces.
Overlap
Repeating a few sentences between neighbouring chunks so ideas are not cut in half.
Contextual Retrieval
Anthropic's technique: prepend an LLM-written note about where a chunk sits in its document before indexing.
Embedding / vector search
Turning text into a list of numbers that captures meaning, and finding chunks with similar numbers.
BM25
Classic keyword-ranking formula that scores documents by matching words, weighting rare words more.
Hybrid search
Running keyword and vector search together and merging results.
RRF (Reciprocal Rank Fusion)
A simple way to merge ranked lists: each item scores 1/(k + rank) in each list, summed.
Reranker / cross-encoder
A model that reads the query and a candidate together and outputs a relevance score; slower but more accurate than vectors.
Top-k
The number of results kept at a stage (e.g. top 20).
Query rewriting / multi-query / decomposition
Using an LLM to improve, multiply or split the user's question into better searches.
HyDE
Hypothetical Document Embeddings: generate a fake answer and search with it.
Knowledge graph
Data stored as entities (nodes) and relationships (edges).
GraphRAG
RAG that retrieves from a knowledge graph or graph community summaries rather than only text chunks (Microsoft Research popularised the term).
Multi-hop question
A question that needs chaining facts from several sources.
Recall@k, MRR, faithfulness
Eval metrics from drop 3: did we find it, how high was it ranked, is the answer supported by the context.
Semantic ranker
Azure AI Search's built-in reranking feature.
