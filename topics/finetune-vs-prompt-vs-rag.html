Fine-tuning vs prompting vs RAG: when each one is the right tool

Track: AI/LLM systems. Builds on drop 3 (RAG evals), drop 4 (improving RAG) and drop 5 (long context, CAG, agentic RAG).
Every customer conversation about an LLM project eventually hits the same fork: "Should we fine-tune our own model?" Most of the time the honest answer is "not yet". This topic gives you the decision rule and the words to defend it.

What it is and the problem it solves

There are three levers for making a general model good at a specific job. Prompting changes what you tell the model on each request (instructions, examples, output format). RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) changes what the model can see on each request by fetching relevant documents and pasting them into the prompt. Fine-tuning changes the model itself by training it further on many examples, so the behaviour is baked into its weights.

The problem: teams reach for the most expensive lever first. Fine-tuning feels like "making it ours", but it is slow to iterate, needs hundreds to thousands of clean examples, must be redone when the base model upgrades, and is bad at keeping facts current. The core rule: RAG fixes what the model knows, fine-tuning fixes how the model behaves, prompting is the first try for both.

How it works

Notice that the three are not rivals: they sit at different points of the same pipeline. Prompting and RAG change the input per request; fine-tuning swaps the model box for a specialised one. Mature systems often use all three.

Everyday comparison: a new hire at a hospital
Prompting is the shift briefing you give every morning ("today, write discharge notes in this format"). RAG is handing them the patient's file and the hospital handbook to read while they work (open-book exam). Fine-tuning is sending them through a six-month residency: they come back thinking and writing like a specialist, but they still need the patient's file, because training does not tell them what happened this morning.

When to use / when not to use

Solid arrows = yes, dashed = no. Also fine-tune when cost or latency at high volume matters: a tuned small model can replace a big model with a 3-page prompt.

SituationPickWhy
HR policy bot, policies change monthlyRAGFacts change; you need citations for trust
Always output a strict JSON claim schema in the insurer's wordingPrompt first, then fine-tune if errors persistBehaviour/format problem, not a knowledge problem
Classify 2 million support tickets a day into 40 categoriesFine-tune a small modelNarrow task, huge volume: cheaper and faster per call

Pilot in 2 weeks with no labelled dataPrompt + RAGFine-tuning needs data and time you do not have

Trade-offs: prompting is cheapest to change but costs tokens on every call; RAG adds an index to run, retrieval quality to evaluate and latency; fine-tuning adds training cost, a dataset to curate, a model version to host and re-tune whenever the base model improves, and can quietly lose general ability (catastrophic forgetting).

Real-world scenarios (verified)

Morgan Stanley (RAG-first)
Morgan Stanley Wealth Management built 'AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant' on GPT-4 over its internal research and documents, driven by evals. Per OpenAI's customer story they went 'from being able to answer 7,000 questions to ... any question from a corpus of 100,000 documents' by improving retrieval, and over 98% of advisor teams use it. Knowledge problem, so retrieval + evals, not a new model.
Harvey (fine-tuning when it truly pays)
Harvey, the legal AI company, worked with OpenAI on a custom-trained case-law model, adding the equivalent of about 10 billion tokens of case law (starting with Delaware, then all U.S. case law). In side-by-side tests, lawyers preferred the custom model's output over GPT-4 97% of the time. Deep domain reasoning at scale justified training, and it still cites cases.
Why it matters on the job
As an FDE you will be asked 'can we fine-tune on our data?' in week one. The architect answer is: show the eval baseline with prompting + RAG, name the specific failure fine-tuning would fix, and estimate dataset, cost and re-tuning burden. That saves the customer months.

Pattern card: choose the lightest lever

What it is
Escalate from prompting to RAG to fine-tuning only when an eval shows the lighter lever cannot fix a named failure.
Metaphor
Briefing, then open-book, then residency training.
Real example
Morgan Stanley (retrieval + evals), Harvey (custom-trained case-law model).
Use when
Any new LLM feature; especially when a customer asks for fine-tuning up front.
Do not use when
You already have strong evidence (eval data) that only a trained model meets the bar, e.g. strict latency on a tiny model.
Trade-offs
Speed and flexibility of prompts/RAG vs consistency and per-call cost of a tuned model.
Instruction to AI / a teammate
Build a 50-case eval set first, get a baseline with prompt + RAG (cited answers), and only propose fine-tuning with the failing cases, dataset size and monthly hosting cost written down.

FDE / architect interview angle

  • A bank wants to fine-tune an LLM on its policies so it 'knows' them. What do you recommend? Strong answer: policies change and need citations, so RAG; fine-tuning bakes in facts that go stale and cannot cite; propose an eval set, a RAG baseline, and fine-tuning only for a measured style/format gap.
  • When is fine-tuning actually worth it? Strong answer: narrow, high-volume tasks where a tuned small model cuts cost/latency; consistent output style or domain language prompts can't hold; enough clean examples; a plan for re-tuning when the base model changes; plus how you would measure the win.

Glossary

Prompt / system prompt
The text instructions sent to the model; the system prompt is the fixed part that sets rules.
Few-shot examples
A handful of worked input/output examples placed in the prompt.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: search your documents, add the hits to the prompt, then generate.
Chunk / top-k
A small piece of a document / the k most relevant pieces returned by search.
Fine-tuning
Further training a model on your own examples so its weights change.
Weights
The billions of numbers inside a model that encode what it has learned.
SFT
Supervised fine-tuning: training on example prompts with ideal answers.
Preference tuning (DPO / RLHF)
Training on pairs of better/worse answers so the model prefers the better style.
Catastrophic forgetting
A tuned model getting worse at general things it used to do well.
Eval set (golden set)
A fixed list of test questions with expected answers used to measure quality.
JSONL
A file with one JSON record per line; the usual format for training examples.
Deployment name
The name under which a specific model version is hosted and called on Azure and other clouds.
Base model
The general model before your customisation.
