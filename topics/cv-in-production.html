Computer vision in production: serving, edge vs cloud, monitoring drift

Track: Computer vision in production.
A CV model that hits a great mAP in a notebook is a fraction of the work. Production asks: where does the model run (camera, factory PC, cloud), how fast must it answer, what happens when there is no internet, how do you update 500 devices, and how do you notice when the world changes and accuracy quietly drops.

What it is and the problem it solves

Serving styleHow it worksTypical use
BatchProcess a folder or bucket of images on a scheduleInsurance claim photos overnight, satellite imagery, document scanning
Real-time API (cloud)App uploads an image, a hosted model endpoint returns predictions in a few hundred msID verification, product photo moderation, a web app feature
Streaming video (cloud or on-site server)Video streams decoded, sampled frames run through the modelRetail analytics, CCTV analytics on a site server
EdgeModel runs on a device next to the camera (GPU module, phone, machine controller)Factory inspection, robots, vehicles, agriculture: needs millisecond decisions or works offline

Drift is the other half: the model was trained on yesterday's world. New camera, new lighting, dust on a lens, a new product variant, a different season: inputs change (data drift) or what counts as a defect changes (concept drift), and accuracy falls without any error in the logs.

How it works: the edge-to-cloud loop

  1. Optimise the model for where it runsExport to a portable format (ONNX), compile for the hardware (TensorRT on NVIDIA, OpenVINO on Intel), and quantise (FP32 to INT8) for speed and size, with an accuracy loss you must measure on your golden set.
  2. Decide locally, report centrallyThe device acts on its own prediction, then sends back metrics (prediction counts, confidence histograms, latency) and a sample of images, especially low-confidence ones.
  3. Watch for drift without labelsLabels arrive late or never, so watch proxies: confidence distribution shifting, class mix changing (suddenly 3x more defects), image statistics (brightness, blur), embedding distance from the training set. Confirm with a small human-reviewed sample each week.
  4. Close the loop (active learning)Hard and drifted examples get labelled and added to training and to the golden set; a new model must beat the old one on the golden set before release.
  5. Roll out like softwareVersion every model, deploy over the air to a canary group of devices, compare metrics, then the fleet; keep one-click rollback (the drop 6 canary idea, applied to devices).
Everyday comparison: a chain of restaurant kitchens.
Each kitchen (edge device) cooks on the spot, because customers cannot wait for food from head office (latency) and the delivery road sometimes floods (no connectivity). Kitchens send head office a few sample plates and daily sales numbers (monitoring samples). When tastes change or a new supplier's tomatoes behave differently (drift), the head chef updates the recipe (retrain), tests it in two branches first (canary), then sends it to all kitchens (OTA rollout).

Trade-offs: edge gives speed, offline operation and privacy but you now run a hardware fleet; cloud is simpler to operate but adds latency, bandwidth cost and a dependency on the network. Many real systems are hybrid: decide at the edge, learn in the cloud.

Pattern card: decide at the edge, learn in the cloud

What it is
Run an optimised model on the device for instant decisions, and ship metrics plus sampled hard images to the cloud to detect drift, relabel, retrain and roll out new versions.
Metaphor
Restaurant kitchens cook locally; head office improves the recipe and sends updates.
Real example
John Deere See & Spray (below).
Use when
Millisecond decisions, unreliable connectivity, heavy video, or data that must stay on site.
Do not use when
Low-volume, latency-tolerant use cases: a cloud endpoint or batch job is far simpler.
Trade-offs
Fleet management, device security and OTA updates in exchange for speed and resilience.
Instruction to AI / a teammate
Export the defect-detection model to ONNX with INT8 quantisation, run it on the line's edge GPU with a confidence threshold, upload all frames under 0.6 confidence plus a 1% random sample to cloud storage, build a weekly drift report on confidence and class mix, and deploy new model versions to two lines as a canary before the fleet.

Real-world scenario and why it matters on the job

John Deere See & Spray (technology from Blue River Technology, which Deere acquired): cameras mounted along the sprayer boom (36 cameras on a 120-foot boom, per Mississippi State University Extension) scan the field while the machine moves at around 12 mph, and onboard processors identify weeds and fire individual nozzles in milliseconds, so herbicide lands only on weeds. This has to be edge inference: there is no time for a cloud round trip at that speed, and fields have poor connectivity. A three-year University of Arkansas field trial found 43-59% less herbicide than spraying the whole field, and also showed the business face of the precision/recall trade-off: the lowest sensitivity setting used the least herbicide but let more weeds survive, increasing the weed seed bank in the soil by 280% per year.

Production lessons visible in the model-year 2027 update (DTN, Jan 2026): Deere moved the four centre cameras from behind the tyres to the front to reduce dust interfering with images (a real input-quality problem, the kind that causes drift), cut boom-mounted processors from up to 10 to three, and upgraded cameras to detect about four times as many small weeds.

Why it matters: CV pilots are some of the clearest FDE wins because the metrics (false rejects, missed defects, chemical or scrap saved) are already tracked by the customer. A computer-vision background plus the edge/cloud and drift story is a strong differentiator in AI architect interviews.

# Python, conceptually: edge inference loop with monitoring hooks
session = onnx_session('defect_v7_int8.onnx')      # optimised model, versioned
for frame in camera.frames():
    label, confidence = session.predict(preprocess(frame))
    actuator.act(label)                             # decide locally, in milliseconds
    metrics.record(label, confidence, model='v7')   # for drift dashboards
    if confidence < 0.6 or random() < 0.01:
        uploader.queue(frame, label, confidence)    # hard + random samples to the cloud
// Java: ONNX Runtime for Java or DJL (Deep Java Library) inside a Spring service
//   that receives images from a factory system; same predict + record + sample pattern.
// TypeScript: onnxruntime-web / onnxruntime-node for in-browser or Node inference,
//   or a Next.js route handler that forwards uploads to a cloud endpoint.
// The point: model file + version, a threshold, and monitoring hooks are what to look for.

FDE / architect interview angle

  • Design a visual inspection system for 50 factory lines. Strong answer: edge inference per line for latency and uptime, optimised models, a cloud loop for monitoring, labelling and retraining, a model registry with canary rollouts, drift detection on confidence and class mix, human review of low-confidence cases, and business metrics (false reject rate, escaped defects) as SLOs (topic 2).
  • Your model's accuracy dropped 10% three months after launch. What happened and what do you do? Strong answer: suspect drift (new camera, lighting, product variant, dirty lens); compare recent input statistics and confidence with training data, pull a labelled sample to confirm, retrain with the new data, gate on the golden set, canary the new model, and add drift alerts so it is caught sooner next time.

Glossary

Inference / serving
Running a trained model to get predictions / making it available to callers.
Edge
Computing on or near the device that produces the data, not in a cloud data centre.
Endpoint
A hosted URL that runs a model on request.
Batch inference
Running predictions on many stored items at once on a schedule.
mAP
Mean average precision: a standard object-detection accuracy metric.
ONNX
Open Neural Network Exchange: a portable model file format.
TensorRT / OpenVINO
NVIDIA's / Intel's tools to compile models for fast inference on their hardware.
Quantisation (INT8)
Storing model numbers as 8-bit integers instead of 32-bit floats for speed and size.
Data drift
Inputs change compared with training data (lighting, camera, season).
Concept drift
The relationship between inputs and the right answer changes (a new definition of a defect).
Confidence score
The model's own probability-like score for its prediction.
Embedding distance
How far new images sit from training images in the model's feature space.
Active learning
Choosing the most useful (hard, uncertain) examples for humans to label next.
Model registry
A versioned store of trained models with metadata and approval status.
OTA update
Over-the-air: pushing new software or models to devices remotely.
Fleet
All the deployed devices you manage.
Precision / recall
Of what you flagged, how much was right / of what was really there, how much you caught.
Golden set
A fixed, trusted labelled test set every new model must pass (drop 3).
